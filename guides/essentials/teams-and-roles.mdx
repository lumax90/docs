PixlHub separates people management into two layers: the **workspace** (also called an organization) that owns projects and members, and the **project-level roles** that decide who labels, who reviews and who manages inside each project.

## Workspaces and organizations

The workspace switcher in the top navigation shows which workspace you are in; everything you see (projects, tasks, team) belongs to the active one. Management lives on the **Organizations** page, reachable via **Manage** on the Dashboard's Team panel.

Each organization has three tabs: **Overview** (member and invite counts), **Members** (the roster with each person's role and join date), and **Storage** (the workspace's data footprint). **Create Organization** starts a fresh workspace, useful for separating clients, departments or experiments with entirely different teams.

## Invite people

From the Members tab, **Invite Member** sends an email invitation with a role attached:

<Frame caption="The invite dialog: email plus role. Invitations show as pending until accepted.">
  <img src="/images/docs/collab/invite-member.jpg" alt="PixlHub Invite Member dialog with email address field and role selection" />
</Frame>

| Organization role | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Owner** | The workspace creator: full control, including the organization itself |
| **Admin** | Manages the workspace: projects, members, settings |
| **Member** | Works inside projects they participate in |

## Project-level roles

Inside a project, capability is scoped further. Your role badge shows in the project header (a project **Manager** sees configuration tabs like Label Schema, Workflow and Settings), while day-to-day work splits along the pipeline:

- **Labelers** pull tasks from the label queue, annotate and submit.
- **Reviewers** act on the review queue with [accept and reject](/guides/quality/review-workflow).
- **Managers** configure the project, watch [performance](/guides/quality/performance-analytics), and can do both of the above.

The [workflow's](/guides/projects/workflow) assignment rule on each Label node ("Anyone", or specific people) is where membership meets the queue: it decides which members may pull which tasks.

## Patterns that keep teams sane

- **One workspace per trust boundary.** Clients or datasets that must never mix belong in separate organizations, not separate projects.
- **Least role by default.** Invite as Member, elevate when responsibility arrives; demoting later is the awkward conversation.
- **Reviewers are not optional at scale.** A second pair of eyes per task is the cheapest quality system that exists; wire it into the workflow rather than trusting discipline.

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can one person belong to several workspaces?">
    Yes; the switcher in the top bar lists every workspace your account belongs to, and your role can differ in each.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do labelers see each other's work?">
    Task assignment controls this in practice: members work the queues their role and the workflow's assignment rules give them, and the Tasks table shows ownership per task.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What happens to someone's tasks when they leave?">
    Their completed history stays on the tasks. In-progress assignments return to the queue for others to pull.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
